# Puget Sound Energy EV & Charging Incentives Guide

Compiled 5 September 2026 from pse.com and Puget Sound Energy's filed electric
tariffs. **Companion to the Seattle City Light guide.** Puget Sound Energy (PSE)
is the investor-owned utility serving much of western Washington, including the
parts of Burien, Renton, SeaTac, Shoreline and Tukwila that Seattle City Light
does **not** bill. Whenever a visitor in those cities turns out to be a PSE
customer, this guide is the one that applies — none of the Seattle City Light
incentives do.

**Time-sensitive as of September 2026:** PSE HELP is paused for the whole of
this month, and the Transportation Emission Reduction Project Grant is closed
until 2027. Both are covered below.

## The headline difference from Seattle City Light: PSE pays for a home charger

Seattle City Light offers **no** residential single-family home EV charger
rebate. Puget Sound Energy does. This is the single most useful thing to tell a
visitor who discovers PSE bills them.

**Up & Go Electric Home Charging rebate — open now:**

- Standard — **up to $300** for a qualifying EV home charger
- Income-qualified — **up to $600** for the charger, **plus up to $2,000 toward
  installation costs** (stated elsewhere as up to 100% of installation)

Eligibility, quoted from PSE's rebate page:

- "Must be installed in a property within PSE's electric service area."
- "Must be a current PSE residential customer, with electric service."
- "Must currently own, lease, or have ordered an EV (electric vehicle) or PHEV
  (plug-in hybrid electric vehicle)."
- "Must purchase, install, and Wi-Fi connect an ENERGY STAR Smart Level 2
  charger that is prequalified by PSE."
- "One rebate per qualified single-family residence within a 5-year window."
- "Must purchase and install a qualifying EV home charger after 01/01/2024."

Apply at https://www.pse.com/en/rebates/ev-home-charger — the page carries a
live "APPLY ONLINE" button and forms in English, Spanish and Vietnamese.

**Trap:** a second live PSE page,
`pse.com/en/pages/electric-cars/interest-list-sign-up`, still says "Level 2
charger rebates are coming soon" and collects an interest list. That page is
stale. The rebate is open and applications are being taken. Do not tell a
visitor to wait.

## PSE Flex EV — getting paid to shift charging

PSE Flex EV is a demand-response programme that pays a residential customer to
charge outside peak events. **$50 on enrolment**, plus **$0.50 for every
kilowatt-hour saved during Flex events**.

Events run 7 a.m.–10 p.m., last 2–4 hours, never fall on holidays, and occur
in two seasons: 1 November–31 March and 1 May–30 September. Rewards are paid
within 4–6 weeks.

To join, a customer needs an AMI meter and either an EV with an active
manufacturer connected-services subscription, or a compatible Level 2 home
charger. Enrol at https://www.pse.com/en/rebates/PSE-flex/flex-ev

**Note on a stale figure:** PSE's older Home Charging overview page still says
"$100 incentive for joining ($50 after May 31)". That page dates from 2024 and
the step-down has already happened. **$50** is the current enrolment reward per
the dedicated Flex EV programme page.

## PSE electric rates — the super off-peak window is the real EV lever

This is where a PSE customer saves the most on charging, and it is better than
anything Seattle City Light offers residentially. All figures below are the
rates in force "Beginning January 29, 2026", taken from PSE's filed tariffs.

**Schedule 7 — standard residential (the default):** basic charge $7.49/month
single phase; energy **$0.141598/kWh** for the first 600 kWh, **$0.161015/kWh**
above 600 kWh.

**Schedule 327 — Time-of-Use with Super Off-Peak (opt-in).** The one to
recommend to an EV owner:

- **Super off-peak — $0.075542/kWh**, every day, year-round, **midnight to
  7 a.m. and 11 p.m. to midnight**
- Winter (1 Oct–31 Mar) weekday peak (7–10 a.m., 5–8 p.m.) — $0.503575/kWh
- Winter weekday off-peak (10 a.m.–5 p.m., 8–11 p.m.) — $0.127088/kWh
- Summer (1 Apr–30 Sep) weekday peak (7–10 a.m., 5–8 p.m.) — $0.271839/kWh
- Summer weekday off-peak — $0.122055/kWh
- Weekends and holidays are off-peak 7 a.m.–11 p.m.

Charging overnight on Schedule 327 costs **$0.075542/kWh against $0.141598/kWh**
on the standard rate — roughly **47% less per kWh**. That is the number to lead
with for a PSE customer who charges at home overnight.

**Schedule 307 — Time-of-Use without the super off-peak tier.** Winter weekday
peak $0.532445/kWh, off-peak $0.108197/kWh; summer weekday peak (5–8 p.m. only)
$0.335186/kWh, off-peak $0.108197/kWh. Better off-peak rate than 327, but no
overnight super off-peak window — for an EV owner charging overnight, 327 is
usually the better fit.

**Both rates are capacity-capped and both caps are small.** Schedule 307 is
"limited to a total of 6,500 Customer accounts"; Schedule 327 is limited to
**2,500 customer accounts**, and PSE has "sole discretion to limit and control
individual Customer participation." PSE's public pages do **not** say whether
either cap is currently full. Tell the visitor to confirm availability through
the enrolment flow or by phone before counting on it.

Not eligible for either: net-metering (solar) customers, Flex Batteries
customers, and customers on Schedule 171.

**Contradiction to be aware of:** PSE's consumer page "when you charge matters"
says *"While we don't offer discounts for charging your electric vehicle during
certain hours, we're exploring different methods and incentives around EV
charging."* That statement is contradicted by PSE's own filed tariffs above.
**Trust the tariff, not that page** — but expect a visitor who read it to
believe no time-of-use option exists.

## PSE multifamily EV charging

For a multifamily property owner or manager in PSE electric territory. Unlike
Seattle City Light's programme, this is not restricted to affordable housing.

- **PSE-owned (turnkey)** — "100 percent of installation and maintenance costs
  up to $10,000 per Level 2 charging port", on a 10-year term
- **Customer-owned** — "50 percent of equipment and installation costs up to
  $2,000 per Level 2 charging port"
- **Empower Mobility (income-qualified / Tribal communities)** — PSE-owned 100%
  up to $10,000 per port; customer-owned **100% up to $4,000 per port**; plus
  shared-mobility vehicle rebates up to $7,500

Applications are reviewed on a monthly evaluation cycle.
https://www.pse.com/en/pages/electric-cars/multifamily-charging

## PSE workplace charging

For a commercial PSE electric account with dedicated EV parking.

- **PSE-owned** — 100% of installation and maintenance "up to $12,000 per Level
  2 charging port for up to 10 ports per property" (so up to $120,000)
- **Customer-owned** — "50 percent of equipment and installation costs up to
  $2,000 per Level 2 charging port for up to 10 ports"
- **Empower Mobility** — 100% up to $4,000 per port, up to 10 ports

Commitments: operate the equipment 10 years, run annual employee surveys, use
hardware from PSE's qualified manufacturer list.

## PSE fleet electrification

For a **non-residential** PSE electric account holder. Transmission-only
customers on Schedules 448/449 are **not** eligible.

- **PSE-owned** — Level 2 up to **$12,000 per port**; DC fast charging up to
  **$125,000 per port**; battery-electric forklift $2,000 per EV
- **Customer-owned** — Level 2 up to **$4,000 per port**; DCFC up to **$60,000
  per port**; forklift $2,000 per EV
- **Combined cap — $250,000 total per charging location**
- **Empower Mobility vehicle rebates** — light-duty (Class 1–2) **$7,500/EV**;
  medium-duty (Class 3–6) up to **$100,000/EV**; heavy-duty (Class 7–8) up to
  **$150,000/EV**
- **Load management (off-peak charging)** — annual incentives of $120 (Level 2),
  $300 (DCFC up to 75 kW), $1,000 (75–150 kW), $2,000 (150 kW+); paid as a
  monthly on-bill credit "between $10 and $165 per charger"

Requirements: procure at least two owned or leased EVs before installation (one
for qualified Empower Mobility applicants), agree to operate the charging
equipment for 10 years, complete annual surveys, and use networked charging
equipment.

**PSE states plainly: "Applications do not guarantee funding."** It is a
prioritised, competitive programme — never tell a business the money is theirs.

## PSE public charging

For a host with controlling interest in a property in PSE electric territory.

- **PSE-owned** — 100% of costs for both Level 2 and DCFC, **no maximum**;
  requires a 10-year minimum lease agreement
- **Customer-owned** — Level 2 up to 50% / **$2,000 per port**; DCFC up to 50% /
  **$40,000 per port**; **$250,000 per project**; service upgrades $20,000 per
  DCFC port and $2,000 per Level 2 port; requires a separate dedicated meter
- **Empower Mobility** — Level 2 up to 100% / $4,000 per port; DCFC up to 100% /
  $80,000 per port, rising to $100,000 per DCFC port in the enhanced tier

Empower Mobility eligibility rests on serving communities rated 9–10 on the
Washington Department of Health Environmental Health Disparities map, Indian
country census tracts, or vulnerable populations. PSE is explicit that
**"Geographic location alone does not qualify a project."**

## Discount public charging — 45% off, but only 200 customers

A PSE residential customer already enrolled in the **Bill Discount Rate** can
get **45% off** PSE's Up & Go Electric public charging network for 12 months:
DC fast charging **$0.23/kWh** (against $0.42 undiscounted) and Level 2
**$0.15/kWh** (against $0.28). An idle fee of $0.40/minute applies 10 minutes
after charging completes.

**"This discount is only available to 200 eligible customers."** PSE's pages do
not say whether the 200 slots are full. Present it as worth applying for, never
as guaranteed.

## Income-qualified help from PSE

**Bill Discount Rate (BDR)** — open. A discount of **5% to 45% per month** on
the bill, scaled to household income and size. Eligibility is household income
at or below **80% of county area median income, or 200% of the federal poverty
level, whichever is greater**. PSE states that "in most cases, no proof of
income is required to apply". Enrolling in BDR is also the gateway to the 45%
public-charging discount above.

**PSE HELP (Home Energy Lifeline Program)** — credits an account **up to
$1,000**, same income test as BDR. **PAUSED RIGHT NOW.** PSE's page states:
*"PSE HELP applications received between September 1, 2026 and September 30,
2026 will not be processed. Please come back to apply for PSE HELP starting
October 1, 2026."* If a visitor asks during September 2026, tell them to apply
from 1 October — an application filed now is discarded, not queued.

**Efficiency Boost** — an income-based uplift that raises rebate amounts across
PSE's catalogue, and it explicitly **includes EV home chargers**. Worth naming
to any income-qualified visitor buying a charger.

**Past Due Bill Forgiveness** — open. **Home Weatherization Assistance** — open,
but PSE's own page warns of long wait times.

Income thresholds vary by county (net monthly income). Examples from PSE's
income guidelines page: King and Snohomish counties, 1-person household
**$7,071/month**, 4-person **$10,096/month**; Pierce County 1-person
$5,642/month, 4-person $8,054/month; Kitsap 1-person $5,804/month.
https://www.pse.com/en/account-and-billing/assistance-programs/Income-Guidelines

## What is NOT available from PSE right now

- **Transportation Emission Reduction (TER) Project Grant — CLOSED.** The 2026
  application window has closed and reopens in 2027. TER **Grant Matching** and
  **Grant Writing** support remain open year-round subject to funding.
- **V2X / vehicle-to-everything — not open.** PSE states V2X "is currently not"
  available as a customer programme.
- **The ChargeScape vehicle-to-home demonstration** (announced 18 March 2026,
  with Ford, Kia and Wallbox) and the **Electric Era battery-backed DC fast
  charging demonstration** (28 April 2026) are named pilot partnerships, **not
  programmes a customer can sign up for**. Do not present them as available.
- **The low-income Bill Protection pilot tied to Schedule 317 ended 30 September
  2025** and is closed to new participants.

## Pending: PSE's 2026 general rate case could raise EV charging rates

PSE filed a general rate case around 27 February 2026 (Washington UTC dockets
260005/260006). It includes the **Transportation Electrification rate Schedule
558**, with a requested **31.56% increase in 2027**. Public hearings were
scheduled for 29 September and 7 October 2026.

Nothing is decided. Do not tell a visitor rates are going up — but for a
business weighing a fleet-charging commitment on Schedule 558, the filing is
worth knowing about and worth verifying with PSE directly.

## Service territory — and the Seattle boundary problem

PSE describes its service area **by county, not by city**, at
https://www.pse.com/en/Customer-Service/pse-locations-2 . Note that PSE serves
**gas** in some areas where it does not serve **electricity**; only electric
service matters for everything in this guide.

For the shared cities — **Burien, Renton, SeaTac, Shoreline, Tukwila** — the
scrape found **no clean public lookup tool** on pse.com that resolves a single
address to PSE or Seattle City Light. The honest answer to give a visitor is:
**read the utility bill.** The name on the electric bill decides which of these
two guides applies, and no ZIP-code table is reliable in those cities.

## What EVan must not claim about Puget Sound Energy

- **Do not** apply Seattle City Light programmes to a PSE customer, or PSE
  programmes to a City Light customer. Ask which utility bills them first. In
  Burien, Renton, SeaTac, Shoreline and Tukwila this is not guessable.
- **Do not** say PSE's home charger rebate is coming soon. It is open. A stale
  PSE interest-list page says otherwise and is wrong.
- **Do not** quote $100 for the Flex EV enrolment bonus. It is $50 now.
- **Do not** promise a spot on Schedule 307 or 327. They are capped at 6,500 and
  2,500 accounts and PSE does not publish how full they are.
- **Do not** promise the 45% public charging discount. It is capped at 200
  customers with no published availability.
- **Do not** tell anyone to apply to PSE HELP during September 2026 — those
  applications are discarded. Applications reopen 1 October 2026.
- **Do not** present the TER Project Grant as open. It is closed until 2027.
- **Do not** offer the ChargeScape or Electric Era demonstrations as customer
  programmes.
- **Do not** tell a fleet customer their incentive is secured. PSE states
  "Applications do not guarantee funding."
- **Do not** repeat the federal charger tax-credit deadline shown on PSE's EV
  FAQ page — it still cites 31 December 2021 and is years out of date. Federal
  incentives are out of scope here; send the visitor to verify separately.

Always tell the visitor to confirm current amounts, caps and availability with
PSE before spending money, and send them to the programme page rather than to a
summary.

# Seattle City Light EV & Charging Incentives Guide

Source document for EVan's knowledge base (`etus_kb_documents`). Upload or paste
this into the admin CMS → **EVan knowledge base** → new document, source slug

because retrieval returns chunks, not the whole file.

Compiled 5 September 2026 from seattle.gov/city-light and City Light's own
programme documents. **This guide supersedes the Seattle City Light portions of
the older "Washington State & Seattle City Light" document compiled 28 July
2026.** That document's Seattle figures were taken from a manual City Light has

The Washington State and federal programmes (WSDOT NEVI, WSDOT Green
Transportation Capital Grants, WAZIP) are **not** re-verified here; keep using

## Which City Light document is authoritative

Seattle City Light's charger incentives are governed by the **Transportation
Electrification Incentive Participation Agreement**, a 38-page PDF whose every
page footer reads **"UPDATED NOVEMBER 10, 2025"**.

https://www.seattle.gov/documents/Departments/CityLight/Energy/TEParticipationAgreement.pdf

It contains four incentive tables:

- Table 1 — on-road and non-road EV charger incentive structure (Business EV Charging)
- Table 2 — Multifamily EV Charging Program incentive structure
- Table 3 — Public Charging Program, Charger Incentive
- Table 4 — Public Charging Program, Make-Ready Incentive

**A superseded document is still live and still ranks in search.** The "2025 EV
Charger Rebate Application" (`TERebatesApplication.pdf`) is stamped "UPDATED MAY
14, 2025" and states that DC fast-charger rebate values were lowered to match
Level 2 and that all other offerings were merged into a single tier. **Those May
2025 changes were reversed by the November 2025 revision.** Anyone quoting the
May 2025 PDF — including third-party incentive aggregators — is quoting numbers
City Light no longer uses. If a visitor cites figures matching that document,
tell them it is superseded.

## What changed since the July 2026 guide

Three statements in the older knowledge-base document are now **wrong**:

1. **"The Multifamily program is affordable-housing only."** No longer true.
   City Light's Multifamily EV Charging page states: *"Effective Nov. 12, 2025,
   we have reactivated and updated incentive offerings for market-rate
   properties."* Market-rate multifamily properties can apply again.
2. **"Fleet Electrification Program"** is the old name. The programme is now the
   **Business EV Charging Program**, and the old fleet URL now serves the same
   content as the new one. The contact address is `businessEVcharging@seattle.gov`.
3. **"Effective May 14, 2025, DCFC rebate values were lowered to match Level 2
   and other tiers merged."** Superseded. The November 10, 2025 agreement
   restores the separate DC fast-charging tiers ($100,000 Tier 1 / $200,000
   Tier 2 per site) and restores the two-tier structure.

Two statements are **still true** and should keep being said:

- City Light has **no** general residential single-family-home EV charger
  rebate. Confirmed again on 5 September 2026.
- Overburdened communities are those scoring **9 or 10** on the Washington State
  Department of Health Environmental Health Disparities map. (City Light's web
  pages phrase it as "a 9.0 or higher rank"; the Participation Agreement says "a
  score of 9 or 10". Same threshold.)

## Seattle City Light — no home charger rebate for a single-family house

Seattle City Light does **not** offer a rebate for a homeowner installing a
Level 2 charger at a single-family house. This was re-checked on 5 September
2026 against City Light's own Personal Vehicle Charging page
(seattle.gov/city-light/residential-services/home-energy-solutions/electric-vehicles),
which carries no dollar figure for a home charger.

Some third-party aggregator sites list a flat $500–$700 Seattle City Light home
charger rebate. That figure appears nowhere in City Light's own pages or its
Participation Agreement. Treat it as unverified and do not quote it.

What a Seattle City Light residential customer **can** use: the opt-in
Residential Time-of-Use rate (below), the Utility Discount Program if
income-qualified (below), and — if they live in a building of five or more units
— their property's eligibility for the Multifamily EV Charging Program, which
the building's governing body applies for, not the resident.

## Multifamily EV Charging Program — amounts (current, Nov 2025)

For Seattle City Light account holders whose property has **at least five
units**. The property's governing body (HOA, board, or committee) applies — not
an individual resident. Both market-rate and affordable properties are eligible
as of 12 November 2025.

**Tier 1 — market-rate properties:**

- 120V outlet (assigned or shared parking) — **75% of project costs, up to $13,000 per site**
- 240V outlet (shared parking only) — **100% of project costs, up to $15,000 per site**
- Level 2 wall- or pedestal-mounted (shared parking only) — **50% of project costs, up to $25,000 per site**

**Tier 2 — affordable housing properties:**

- 120V outlet and 240V outlet — **100% of project costs, up to $15,000 per site**
- Level 2 — **100% of project costs, up to $50,000 per site**

Affordable housing means rental properties with at least 40% of units restricted
to 60% AMI for 40 years, or owned units with at least 40% restricted to 80% AMI
for at least 50 years.

Important limit: for 120V and 240V outlet projects the incentive covers only the
cost of installing the outlet — **it does not cover the charging cord or
equipment**.

Contact: `MultifamilyEV@seattle.gov`, (206) 801-2890.

## Business EV Charging Program — amounts (current, Nov 2025)

Formerly the Fleet Electrification Program. For Seattle City Light commercial
(non-residential) account holders. Applications are accepted **first-come,
first-served**, and site caps reset on a calendar-year basis.

**Tier 1 — standard applicants** (not in an overburdened community, not a
qualifying small business/WMBE/tribal entity):

- Forklift chargers, and electric standby truck refrigeration units — **50% up to $7,500 per site**
- 240V outlet & portable EVSE (6.6 kW) — **100% up to $15,000 per site**
- Hardwired Level 2 EVSE (6.6 kW) — **50% up to $25,000 per site**
- DC fast charging equipment (50 kW) — **50% up to $100,000 per site**

**Tier 2 — overburdened community, WMBE, small business, small nonprofit, or
tribal entity:**

- Forklift chargers, standby truck refrigeration units, and 240V outlet — **100% up to $15,000 per site**
- Hardwired Level 2 EVSE (6.6 kW) — **100% up to $50,000 per site**
- DC fast charging equipment (50 kW) — **100% up to $200,000 per site**

A small business qualifies at 50 or fewer employees, or under $7M annual gross
revenue.

**Programme-wide cap:** a participant and its affiliated entities can receive a
maximum of **$750,000 in incentives aggregated annually** across multiple sites,
projects, or programmes.

Contact: `businessEVcharging@seattle.gov`. Documents were historically sent to
`eFleets@seattle.gov`.

## Public Charging Program — amounts (new in Nov 2025)

This programme did not exist in the May 2025 document — it was added by the
November 10, 2025 revision, and it pays twice: once for the charger, once for
the electrical make-ready work. For businesses and property owners in Seattle
City Light territory hosting publicly accessible charging.

**Eligibility:** minimum of **four charging ports per site**, and the chargers
must be publicly accessible **7 days a week, 12 or more hours daily**.

**Charger Incentive** — Tier 1 (non-overburdened) / Tier 2 (overburdened):

- Level 2 EVSE (6.6 kW) — 50% up to **$2,500** / 100% up to **$5,000** per site
- DC fast charging (100 kW) — 50% up to **$20,000** / 100% up to **$40,000**
- DC fast charging (150 kW) — 50% up to **$50,000** / 100% up to **$80,000**
- DC fast charging with battery storage (150 kW) — 50% up to **$125,000** / 100% up to **$150,000**

**Make-Ready Incentive** — Tier 1 / Tier 2:

- Level 2 EVSE (6.6 kW) — 50% up to **$5,000** / 100% up to **$10,000** per site
- DC fast charging (100 kW) — 50% up to **$50,000** / 100% up to **$150,000**
- DC fast charging (150 kW) — 50% up to **$100,000** / 100% up to **$200,000**
- DC fast charging with battery storage — 50% up to **$25,000** / 100% up to **$50,000**

City Light's own flyer summarises the combined opportunity as **"up to
$150,000–$280,000 in financial incentives"** per project, which remains an
accurate headline. Note that the flyer and the Participation Agreement disagree
on the battery-storage make-ready row; the Participation Agreement is the
governing document.

Contact: `SCL_PublicCharging@seattle.gov`, (206) 801-2890.

## EV Charging Installer Program — for contractors, deadline Nov 30 2026

Seattle City Light reimburses electrical contractors for certification, to widen
the pool of qualified charger installers.

- WMBE registration fee rebate — **$50 to $100**
- EV Infrastructure Training Program (EVITP) training and exam fee — **$275**
- EVITP certification rebates for **up to four staff members per firm**

All three run **through 30 November 2026, or until capacity is reached**. This
deadline is close — flag it to any contractor who asks.

Aimed at women- and/or minority-owned firms, using state or city self-reported
WMBE certification.

## Residential Time-of-Use rate — the real home-charging lever in Seattle

Because there is no home charger rebate, the Time-of-Use rate is the main way a
Seattle City Light residential customer lowers EV charging cost. It launched in
May 2026 and is **opt-in**.

- **Peak** — 5 PM to 9 PM, Monday through Saturday, excluding observed holidays
- **Mid-peak** — 6 AM to 5 PM and 9 PM to midnight, Monday through Saturday; all day (6 AM to midnight) on Sundays and observed holidays
- **Off-peak** — midnight to 6 AM, every day

Charging overnight puts the whole session in the off-peak window. To enrol, a
customer needs an advanced (AMI) meter whose number is seven digits starting
with "2", and an active Utility Services Website profile. The rate is compatible
with the Utility Discount Program, Budget Billing and Green Up. **Net-metering
(solar) customers are currently excluded.** A customer who leaves the rate must
wait 12 months to re-enrol.

## Income-qualified help — Utility Discount Program and Emergency Bill Assistance

These are not EV programmes, but they change what a household can afford and are
frequently missed.

**Utility Discount Program (UDP)** — a **60% discount on Seattle City Light
bills** and 50% off Seattle Public Utilities bills. 2026 gross income limits: 1
person $4,269/month ($51,228/year); 2 people $5,582/month ($66,984/year); 3
people $6,895/month ($82,740/year); 4 people $8,209/month ($98,508/year); 5
people $9,522/month ($114,264/year); 6 people $10,836/month ($130,032/year).
Each additional household member above ten adds $247/month.

**Emergency Bill Assistance (EBA)** — up to **$710 per calendar year** toward a
past-due balance; households with children under 18 may receive up to **$1,420
per calendar year**. Triggered by an urgent or final shutoff notice, or a
past-due balance of $250 or more. The income limits are higher than UDP's: 1
person $4,878/month; 2 people $6,379/month; 4 people $9,382/month.

Apply through utilityassistance.seattle.gov.

## Clean Fuel Standard — do not promise cash back

Seattle City Light generates Washington Clean Fuel Standard credits when its
electricity is used for transportation. **That credit revenue does not go to
individual customers or charging hosts as cash or a rebate.** City Light's page
says credits are *"reinvested by City Light into large-scale initiatives"* —
grid and charging infrastructure.

More than that: Section 7 of the Transportation Electrification Incentive
Participation Agreement has a participant **assign the Clean Fuel Standard
credits generated by an incentivised charger to Seattle City Light**. So a
business taking a City Light charger incentive is giving up those credits, not
earning them. Never tell a visitor they can collect both.

## Service territory — check the bill before quoting anything

Seattle City Light is a municipally owned utility. Its own pages state it serves
a population of about **940,000**, delivering to **over 503,000 customer
premises across 131 square miles**, with 16 major substations. Its "what we do"
page gives **419,601 residential and 50,779 non-residential customers**. (The
older knowledge-base document's "465,000 residential and 52,600 commercial" is
out of date.)

Every Seattle City Light programme above applies **only to addresses actually
billed by Seattle City Light**. Burien, Renton, SeaTac, Shoreline and Tukwila
are split between City Light and Puget Sound Energy. A customer must confirm the
provider on their own utility bill before applying — a Puget Sound Energy
address in a shared ZIP code gets none of these incentives and should be pointed
to PSE's own programmes.

The ZIP-code appendix in the older Washington guide is still usable for a first
guess, but the bill is what decides.

## What EVan must not claim about Seattle City Light

- **Do not** say City Light offers a home EV charger rebate for a single-family
  house. It does not, and the $500–$700 figure on aggregator sites is unverified.
- **Do not** say the Multifamily program is affordable-housing only. Market-rate
  properties were reactivated effective 12 November 2025.
- **Do not** call it the Fleet Electrification Program. It is the Business EV
  Charging Program.
- **Do not** quote the May 14 2025 figures — single merged tier, DCFC lowered to
  Level 2 rates. Those were reversed in November 2025, and the PDF carrying them
  is still online.
- **Do not** promise Clean Fuel Standard credit revenue to a customer or a
  charging host. Incentivised chargers assign those credits to City Light.
- **Do not** state whether funds remain today. Business EV Charging is
  first-come first-served with limited funds and annual site caps; only City
  Light can say what is left.
- **Do not** apply these to a non-City-Light address. Ask whether the visitor is
  billed by Seattle City Light before quoting any figure here.
- **Do not** promise a federal charger or purchase credit alongside these. Ask
  the visitor to verify current federal status separately; this guide does not
  cover it.

Always tell the visitor to confirm current amounts and availability with the
programme before spending money, and send them to the programme page rather than
to a summary.

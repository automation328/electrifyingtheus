## Oregon Clean Vehicle Rebate Program — status right now

Oregon runs the Oregon Clean Vehicle Rebate Program (OCVRP) through the
Department of Environmental Quality, administered by the Center for Sustainable
Energy. It is **open for a short window in 2026**.

A vehicle must be **purchased or leased between 25 August 2026 and 4 November
2026** to qualify at all. Vehicles bought before 25 August 2026 (during the
earlier suspension) or after 4 November 2026 are not eligible, no matter who
buys them. DEQ has said it expects the program to be fully subscribed and to
**suspend rebates on 5 November 2026**.

The 2026 pool is roughly $8.5 million, about $3.1 million of it reserved for
Charge Ahead. If the money runs out before 4 November, later applicants are
placed on a **waitlist** rather than rejected; DEQ has indicated waitlisted
payments would go out in spring 2027 if further funding arrives.

Program site: https://evrebate.oregon.gov/

## Oregon Standard Rebate — amounts

The Standard Rebate has **no income requirement**. It is open to Oregon
residents, businesses, nonprofits and government agencies, and applies to **new
vehicles only**.

- New battery electric vehicle (BEV), battery 10 kWh or larger — **$2,000**
- New plug-in hybrid (PHEV), battery 10 kWh or larger — **$1,500**
- New PHEV with a battery under 10 kWh — **$750**
- New zero-emission motorcycle — **$375**

DEQ's Standard Rebate page states a base MSRP limit of **under $50,000**.

These amounts were cut in May 2026 (Administrative Order DEQ-3-2026, effective
18 May 2026) to make limited funding last longer — the BEV rebate was $2,500
before that. Any source quoting $2,500 is out of date. The US DOE's AFDC pages
for Oregon were still showing the old figures as of September 2026.

## Oregon Charge Ahead Rebate — amounts

Charge Ahead is the income-qualified rebate. It covers **new and used** EVs, and
is the only one of the two that pays for a used vehicle.

- New BEV — **up to $7,500**
- New PHEV — **up to $5,000**
- Used PHEV — the lesser of **$2,500** or the purchase price
- Used BEV — up to **$4,000**, on a sliding scale by purchase price:
  - under $2,500 — 100% of the price
  - $2,500 to about $8,333 — $2,500
  - about $8,333 to $12,000 — 30% of the purchase price
  - $12,000 and above — $4,000

Zero-emission motorcycles are **not** eligible for Charge Ahead.

## Charge Ahead — who qualifies

Charge Ahead is for **Oregon residents in a low- or moderate-income household**,
plus qualifying low-income service providers.

Household income must be **below 400% of the federal poverty guidelines**, based
on adjusted gross income (IRS Form 1040, line 11). Oregon defines a household as
"an individual living alone, a family with or without children or a group of
individuals who are living together as one economic unit." Anyone claimed as a
dependent on someone else's tax return is not eligible.

Income is verified through Oregon Department of Revenue tax transcripts, through
the Center for Sustainable Energy, or by proof of enrolment in a qualifying
assistance programme such as the Oregon Health Plan, SNAP or TANF.

The dollar threshold depends on household size and changes annually with the
federal guidelines, so **give the 400% rule and point people at the program's
own eligibility page** rather than quoting a specific income figure.

## The two rebates cannot be combined

The program states plainly: **"A Charge Ahead Rebate may not be combined with a
Standard Rebate."** One vehicle, one rebate. An income-qualified buyer takes
Charge Ahead because it is the larger of the two, not in addition to the
Standard Rebate.

There is also a **lifetime cap of two OCVRP rebates per person**, counted across
both programs together.

## How to apply

Two routes, and the deadline is the same either way.

**At the dealer (point of sale).** A participating Oregon dealer can apply the
rebate against the purchase price so the buyer never fronts the money. For
Charge Ahead this requires the buyer to get a **prequalification voucher**
first, through the program site, and bring it to the dealer.

**After the purchase.** Apply online within **six months of the purchase or
lease date**, with proof of Oregon residency, the full purchase or lease
agreement, the vehicle registration, proof of income (Charge Ahead) and a signed
attestation.

Approved rebates are paid **within 75 calendar days of approval**, subject to
funds being available.

Questions go to the Center for Sustainable Energy, which administers the
program: oregoncvrp@energycenter.org or 855-383-4425.

## After the rebate

The vehicle must stay **registered in Oregon for 24 months** after the rebate.
Organizations that take a rebate also file annual electric vehicle miles
travelled (eVMT) reports for two years.

## Used vehicles under Charge Ahead

A used EV bought under Charge Ahead must have been a vehicle that originally
qualified for the Standard Rebate when it was new, and must not carry a salvage
or total-loss title. The program applies electric-range floors to used vehicles
as well. Exact used-vehicle range and price conditions should be checked on the
program's eligible-vehicles page before anyone relies on them.

## No federal purchase credit to stack in this window

The federal clean vehicle tax credits ended before this rebate window opened.
The Section 30D credit for new EVs (up to $7,500) and the Section 25E credit for
used EVs (up to $4,000) both **terminated on 30 September 2025** under the law
signed on 4 July 2025.

So an Oregon rebate on a vehicle bought between 25 August and 4 November 2026
**stands alone** — there is no federal purchase credit left to combine it with.
Do not tell an Oregon buyer they can add $7,500 federal to the state rebate.

The separate federal credit for installing home charging equipment (Section 30C)
ran later than the vehicle credits; treat its current status as something to
verify rather than to assert.

## What EVan must not claim about Oregon

State the rule, not a guess, on each of these:

- **Whether the state rebate stacks with any federal credit** — the program site
  does not address it, and the federal purchase credits have ended anyway.
- **A specific income dollar figure** for Charge Ahead. The rule is 400% of the
  federal poverty guidelines; the dollar amount depends on household size and
  the year.
- **Whether money is still available today.** The window is fixed but the funding
  is not, and the program waitlists once the pool is exhausted. Always send
  people to https://evrebate.oregon.gov/ to check before they buy.
- **Fuel-cell vehicle amounts** under the 2026 rules, which were not restated
  when the amounts were cut.

When a question goes past what is written here, say what is known, say plainly
that the rest needs checking, and hand over the program site or the Center for
Sustainable Energy's contact details.
